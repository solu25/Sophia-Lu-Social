00 · The problem

Manual research was killing my momentum.

Finding and vetting startup founders was my job. Every day: open Sales Navigator, search, click through profiles, pull up their websites, judge whether the design was strong or weak, check them against our ideal client profile, and decide whether they were worth reaching out to.

For ten people, that ate up an entire day. Clicking, scrolling, switching tabs, copy-pasting, ranking. All of it manual, all of it repetitive — and at the end I still had not sent a single message.

Ten-plus clicks across six websites, per company
  • Search
  • Click through to the venture
  • Click through to the startup
  • Export from the startup app
  • Check the website and LinkedIn
  • Connect with the founders

Five to ten minutes per company, before any judgement.

The bottleneck was not the outreach. It was the research that came before it.

If we can speed up design and development, why not the research?

The path I was walking by hand for every single founder.

01 · The build

Scout on OpenClaw.

I hosted OpenClaw on Railway so I could reach Scout from anywhere without keeping a computer running. The setup took time: connecting Railway and OpenClaw, getting the environment variables right, working out how all the configuration files fit together.

OpenClaw runs on a set of markdown files, each with a specific job. Getting them right took a full day of figuring out what belonged where, and how to write instructions an AI agent would actually follow.

02 · Seeing it run

A briefing, delivered to my phone.

I ask for a company. Scout comes back with what it is, in plain language, who built it, and what is worth knowing before I write to them.

One briefing, start to finish, in the time it takes to read it.

What actually changed.

The time is the obvious part. The quieter change is that the research now happens whether or not I feel like doing it that week — and I spend the hour I get back on the part only I can do, which is the message itself.

04 · What I learned

Building an AI agent takes more time than you expect.

The configuration files are the hard part

Installing OpenClaw was hard. The harder part was working out which instructions belong in which file. Get that wrong and the agent behaves inconsistently no matter how good your prompts are.

Version one is just the starting point

Launching Scout was not the finish line. The real refinement happens through use, and you will not know what needs fixing until the agent is live.

Expect to iterate after every session

The instructions you write on day one are not the ones you have on day thirty. Every session through Telegram turns up something to tighten, add or remove.

Connect the tools before you write the instructions

Plugins and skills have to be connected in Railway as environment variables before you reference them anywhere in the configuration files. Tools first, instructions second.

A well-configured agent is surprisingly consistent. Getting it configured is the whole job.
