Floor 02 · Growth workshop · PMMCA

How do you turn someone’s entire career into something people want to buy?

PMMCA had the audience and the content. What it was missing was a path that turned interested people into paying members who finished what they started.

My role: GTM and conversion partner. Offer and pricing work, sales page design, member journey mapping, and the proposal for a guided member experience. Harvey owned the course content and made the final calls.

00 · The problem

Direct outreach converted at 36.8%. The free funnel converted at 4.4%.

Harvey already had an audience: courses, a Circle community, a newsletter and an established presence on LinkedIn. The audience was never the issue. Turning it into paying members was.

In an April working session, Harvey reported that roughly 80 to 90% of sales came through his own manual outreach, and that around 200 to 300 visits from paid LinkedIn promotion had produced no purchases. The membership data showed the same pattern.

19 → 7founding members contacted directly, then paid · 36.8%
113 → 5free LinkedIn for PMMs members, then paid · 4.4%

People who heard from Harvey bought. People who found PMMCA on their own mostly didn’t. Reach and sales were running as two separate systems, and every sale depended on one person’s time.

Sources: April working session; membership analytics from 11 goal responses.

01 · Where members stalled

Members were told what existed, not what was right for them.

I mapped the member experience from first discovery to renewal: ten stages, and at each one what members did, what they were thinking, how they felt, and where it broke.

The pattern was consistent. Members joined, got shown everything at once, and were left to work out what applied to them.

“Which of these is actually relevant to me?”

“I get a DM about renewal. I’m not sure it’s worth it yet.”

Renewal depended on Harvey messaging members himself about 30 days before the annual charge. The same founder dependency from section 00 showed up again at the other end of the journey.

PMMCA has strong content and community. The missing layer is guidance.

02 · What I changed on the page

A buyer should know which path is theirs before they pay.

The same problem showed up at the point of purchase. PMMCA’s courses and community were hard to tell apart, so a buyer couldn’t answer “which one is for me?” without Harvey explaining it personally. I made the differences visible instead of making every tier sound maximally valuable.

1

Three paths: Career Builder at $1,497, Job Seeker at $997, and Coaching & Community at $499 a year. Every path includes the same access to Harvey, the community and the live sessions. Only the courses differ, and the comparison table says so directly.

1 Annual-only benefits, labelled as annual-only

The quarterly 1:1 sessions with Harvey and the KPI Toolkit only come with annual membership. They’re now marked with an asterisk, so a monthly buyer doesn’t assume they’re included.

2 Access to Harvey, stated accurately

The hero said every pathway included direct access to Harvey. A footnote now says which plans actually include it.

03 · What I proposed next

Same content. A clearer path.

Harvey had already built the pieces. The proposal was to connect them into one guided journey, so each member knows what’s relevant to their goal, what to do next, and whether they’re making progress.

The guided journey, in order
  • Join and set a goal. A short survey at signup captures your role and what you want, and the answers shape everything after.
  • Follow your path. A recommended sequence from the existing library, not the whole library at once.
  • Learn, then apply. Each lesson comes with a prompt to use it in your current role that week.
  • Get feedback. Bring the work to Office Hours or share it in the community.
  • See progress. Lessons completed, work applied, milestones reached.
  • Continue. Renewal comes with a progress recap, so staying is based on what you’ve done.

04 · Outcome

What shipped, and what’s next

The pricing and access changes on the sales page shipped. The guided member journey and the curriculum were proposed to Harvey.

Whether the changes improved conversion, completion or renewal isn’t known yet. What the work did produce is a baseline to measure against.

MetricBaseline
Free → paid4.4%

Course completion~5%

The first thing I’d track is the gap between the two conversion rates, split by people who came through Harvey’s conversations and people who came in through LinkedIn and the free courses. Closing that gap is how PMMCA stops depending on one person to sell.
